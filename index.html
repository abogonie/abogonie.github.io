Modeling creatine deficiency in the developing human brain.

Cerebral creatine deficiency syndromes (CCDS) are inborn errors of creatine synthesis (AGAT and GAMT) and transport (SLC6A8). Patients present with intellectual disability, epilepsy and developmental delay because the brain cannot make or take up creatine, its energy reserve. I model these disorders in patient-derived and CRISPR-edited iPSCs, NGN2 cortical neurons and cerebral organoids, and pair the models with population genetics.

  • The first human iPSC-derived neuron model of GAMT deficiency.
  • The only platform that combines automated human neuron modeling, drug screening and AAV gene editing for these disorders.

Research

Human models of GAMT deficiency

Neurologic injury in GAMT deficiency is irreversible once symptoms begin, and current treatment with creatine, L-ornithine and sodium benzoate cannot reverse established damage. By the time a child has symptoms, the damage is already done. These models test how creatine deficiency disrupts human neurodevelopment, and whether earlier intervention could prevent it.

Organoids

Cerebral organoids

Patient-derived and CRISPR-edited iPSCs are patterned into cerebral organoids that follow early human cortical development. GAMT-deficient organoids show delayed neuronal maturation, few mature high-energy neurons, and altered stem cell proliferation.

Neurons

Cortical neurons from a GAMT knockout line

A CRISPR GAMT knockout iPSC line shows marked suppression of the creatine pathway and downstream metabolism, partially rescued by 72 hours of creatine supplementation. NGN2-induced cortical neurons from this line are delayed in maturation. This is the first human iPSC-to-neuron model of GAMT deficiency.

Metabolism

Creatine and guanidinoacetate

GAMT transfers a methyl group from S-adenosylmethionine to GAA to form creatine. Loss of function depletes creatine and causes GAA to accumulate. The work compares how each metabolite affects iPSC proliferation and neuronal maturation, with and without a functional pathway.

Automation and screening

Robots feed the cells, software reads the images

My cultures run on a CellXpress.ai automated cell culture system from Molecular Devices, and its images feed directly into IN Carta for analysis. Every plate receives the same handling on the same schedule, which removes operator variability from the measurements. Robots do the feeding and imaging, so every plate is handled the same way.

Gene therapy

AAV gene editing

AAV vectors are used to test gene-based strategies, including for SLC6A8 deficiency, which does not respond to creatine supplementation.

Gene delivery

How an AAV vector can restore GAMT in a neuron

Adeno-associated virus (AAV) vectors are a leading platform for gene delivery [1], and AAV9 crosses the blood-brain barrier to transduce neurons in neonatal mice [7]. The schematic follows one capsid from the neuronal surface to a working GAMT enzyme. A viral shell with no viral genes carries a working copy of GAMT into the neuron so it can make its own creatine.

Population genetics

How common are the cerebral creatine deficiency syndromes?

Earlier estimates relied on known pathogenic variants in mostly European cohorts. We analyzed 535,662 All of Us genomes and 807,162 gnomAD v4 exomes and genomes for variants in GATM (AGAT), GAMT and SLC6A8, and sorted each variant into three groups, from the most certain to the least. Each group is added to the one before it. We counted how many people carry one nonfunctional copy of these genes.

●Preliminary. These results come from a manuscript in preparation. They have not been peer reviewed or published, and the numbers may change.

Confirmed pathogenic

Listed as pathogenic or likely pathogenic in ClinVar, or reported in patients with the disease.

Likely pathogenic

Variants that should stop the gene working (premature stop, frameshift, splice-site or start-loss) but have not been reported in patients.

In silico pathogenic

Missense or splice changes that computer tools (REVEL, SpliceAI) predict to be damaging but nobody has classified yet.

AGAT · GATM1 in 5,600 to 20,600

Carrier frequency. One affected US birth every 35 to 470 years. Carriers have 9.6% lower serum creatinine.

 GAMT1 in 430 to 720

Carrier frequency. Two to five affected US births a year, with more unclassified variants in non-European ancestry groups.

 SLC6A8 · CTD1 in 6,400 to 14,800

Female carrier frequency. About 18 times as many predicted affected births as GAMT deficiency.
