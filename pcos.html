Side project · Women’s health

PCOS genetics in a diverse US population: what is known, and what is not.

Women’s health is understudied. Polycystic ovary syndrome (PCOS) is a common endocrine and metabolic condition with published genetic risk loci. Using electronic health records and genome sequencing from the All of Us Research Program, I am testing whether those loci hold up in a large, diverse US cohort, and I am also evaluating small-molecule interactions and how drugs affect PCOS.

KnownLarge genetic studies have already reported risk loci for PCOS (29 in one study, 94 in another).
TestingDo those loci hold up in a diverse US cohort where PCOS comes from health records?
Not yet knownHow genetic risk relates to age at diagnosis and to co-occurring conditions.
●Preliminary. This is early, unpublished work in progress. It has not been peer reviewed, and conclusions may change.

Where things stand

Known, being tested, and not yet known

Already known

Published risk loci

Two large genetic studies identified 29 and 94 PCOS risk loci.

Being tested

Do they hold up here?

All of Us defines PCOS from electronic health records, not from a research diagnosis. Early, preliminary results: for the variants that could be tested, the large majority point the same way as in the original studies. A polygenic score built from the published variants is associated with PCOS in African, Latino/Admixed American, East Asian and European ancestry groups. The East Asian group is small, so its estimate is imprecise.

Not yet known

Open questions

How genetic risk relates to age at diagnosis and to co-occurring conditions is still being analyzed. Individual loci are underpowered, so none can be called replicated on its own. Effect sizes appear smaller than published, which is expected when published effects are inflated and PCOS is defined differently.

African and Latino/Admixed American groups are ones where PCOS genetics has been studied much less.

How a polygenic score is tested

A score adds up the published risk variants a person carries. If the variants hold up, people with PCOS should tend to score a little higher.

Illustration of the idea, not measured data.

Diverse cohort

Why a diverse cohort matters

PCOS genetics has been studied much less in some groups, including African and Latino/Admixed American populations. All of Us includes people from many ancestry groups, so it can ask whether the published risk loci hold up beyond the groups they were first found in.
