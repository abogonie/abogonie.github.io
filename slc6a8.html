Creatine transport

Creatine transporter deficiency is predicted to be about 18 times more common than GAMT deficiency.

SLC6A8 encodes the transporter that carries creatine into cells, including neurons. It is X-linked, so it mostly affects boys. Unlike AGAT and GAMT deficiency, it does not respond to creatine supplementation, and it is not on the newborn screening panel.

How variants are grouped on this page

Every number below counts variants in three groups, from most certain to least. Each step adds to the one before.

Step 1

Confirmed pathogenic

Listed as pathogenic or likely pathogenic in ClinVar, or reported in patients with the disease.

Step 2 · adds to step 1

Likely pathogenic

Variants that should stop the gene working (a premature stop, frameshift, splice-site or start-loss change) but have not been reported in patients.

Step 3 · adds to steps 1 and 2

In silico pathogenic

Missense or splice changes that computer tools (REVEL and SpliceAI) predict to be damaging, with no clinical classification yet.

Predicted births

Far more boys than the patient numbers suggest

About 130 people with creatine transporter deficiency have been reported, similar to the number with GAMT deficiency. The population data say the two should differ many-fold. Because males have a single X chromosome, a variant carried by a heterozygous female predicts affected sons.

The gap implies that creatine transporter deficiency is likely underdiagnosed. These estimates exclude de novo variants.

Males

Hemizygous males with no recorded diagnosis

26 males in All of Us carried a + In silico variant on their single X chromosome (1 in 7,909). They carried 20 different variants, and 17 of these were not seen in any female. Fewer than 20 males with a + Likely variant had health records, and none had a recorded diagnosis consistent with creatine transporter deficiency, such as intellectual disability, developmental delay, autism or epilepsy.

Some of these variants may be tolerated, and some may be real cases that were never diagnosed. Separating the two is a question for functional testing, which is where iPSC-derived neurons can help.
