Step one of creatine synthesis

AGAT deficiency is the rarest creatine disorder. Carriers are not.

Arginine:glycine amidinotransferase (AGAT, encoded by GATM) makes guanidinoacetate from arginine and glycine. When both copies fail, the brain lacks creatine, but the condition responds to creatine supplementation if it is found early.

Background

What is known

AGAT deficiency is autosomal recessive. In the largest published series there are only 16 patients from 8 families. Because the block is upstream of guanidinoacetate, levels of that metabolite are low rather than high, and creatine supplementation is the main treatment. As in the other creatine disorders, damage that has already occurred cannot be reversed.

A different, dominant condition also involves GATM: a small number of heterozygous missense variants cause a kidney disease (renal Fanconi syndrome) through aggregation of the mutant enzyme in mitochondria of the proximal tubule. None of those 14 published variants were found in either cohort.

How common are pathogenic GATM variants?

Carriers by variant tier

Tier 1 is established pathogenic. Tier 2 adds predicted loss-of-function. Tier 3 adds predicted damaging missense and splice variants.

All of Us and gnomAD v4 agree closely at every tier (p ≥ 0.68), and no single GATM variant was carried by 20 or more All of Us participants.

A measurable effect

Loss-of-function carriers have lower serum creatinine

Creatinine is a breakdown product of creatine, so a gene that makes creatine could plausibly change it. Among 270,383 participants without chronic kidney disease, heterozygous carriers of GATM loss-of-function variants had serum creatinine 9.6% lower than non-carriers (95% CI −15.6% to −3.2%; p = 0.004).

GAMT carriers, who are heterozygous for a defect further down the same pathway, showed no difference. Carriers of rare GATM missense variants had normal rates of chronic kidney disease (10.7% vs 11.1%) and proteinuria (3.4% vs 4.0%).
